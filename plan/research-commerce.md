# IronStone BOSS — Commerce app research

Oct 18, 2026

Deeper research pass on the **IronStone Commerce** app (stores & channels, merchandising — categories, product content, SEO — online orders & carts, channel and marketplace integrations, shipping, payment & tax settings), cataloguing every relevant screen/page found in the reference apps. Companion to [3p-app-reference.md](3p-app-reference.md) and the sibling passes [research-drawers-commons.md](research-drawers-commons.md) / [research-accounting.md](research-accounting.md) / [research-products.md](research-products.md); feeds the design of the Commerce row in [initial-design.md](initial-design.md) plus the store-order face of the shared Customer 360 screen.

**Organization.** Sorted by functional area (not by app): all storefront screens together, all checkout screens together, etc. Within each area, sub-tables per reference app. Style mirrors [research-drawers-commons.md](research-drawers-commons.md).

**Scope.** The e-commerce and channel-management layer: site/store/channel management, merchandising faces, the customer-facing storefront, cart/checkout, online-order administration, integrations, and commerce-adjacent settings. The product-catalog UI itself is out of scope here — it is catalogued in [research-products.md](research-products.md) (referenced below where the commerce face plugs into it). Sales-rep order entry, invoices/bills and accounting live in the other apps' passes.

| --- | --- | --- |

| ERPNext (frappe core) | Single-website assumption in this version: `frappe/website/doctype/website_settings/` is one global settings doctype; `portal_settings`/`portal_menu_item` manage which doctypes the customer portal exposes; no multi-store channel entity | `frappe/frappe/website/doctype/website_settings/`, `frappe/frappe/website/doctype/portal_settings/` |

**BOSS implications.** The Odoo `website` record is the cleanest pattern to lift: BOSS should have a "channel" record that both IronStone Commerce (storefront, POS, portal) and IronStone Sales (per-channel price lists, tax behavior) share, rather than scattering domain/config in a settings page. Dolibarr's separation of CMS site vs customer portal is the fallback for BOSS's Commons-vs-Commerce boundary: site pages are content, the portal is a data surface. AureusERP's grant/revoke portal-access actions are the right auth UX for portal users and belong in IronStone Admin's user screens.

**BOSS implications.** Odoo's "public category separate from internal category" is the single most valuable pattern here: BOSS Products (internal taxonomy) and Commerce (public taxonomy) need different records, and forcing one tree to serve both ends in Odoo-style workarounds. The `website.published.mixin` publish-toggle idea is worth borrowing as a generic "channel-visible" flag BOSS adds to any product/category/blog record. Odoo's `product.document` + ribbon + tag pattern suggests BOSS Product 360 needs a commerce face exposing exactly these storefront-only fields. For SEO, the frappe `website_meta_tag`/`website_route_meta` doctypes are the simplest data model — BOSS should attach meta fields to route records rather than pages.

| AureusERP | `PageResource` Filament CRUD (Schemas/Tables/Pages subtree) for CMS pages plus `PartnerResource` for portal partners; `PortalContributions.php` registers portal-side routes | `plugins/webkul/website/src/Filament/Admin/Resources/PageResource/`, `plugins/webkul/website/src/PortalContributions.php` |

**BOSS implications.** Three storefront models appear: Odoo's "every record is a page via a mixin" (richest, most coupled to the CMS), frappe's "URL→renderer dispatch table" (cleanest separation of routing from content), and Dolibarr's "CMS site with DB pages separate from the portal" (simplest to bolt onto an ERP). BOSS is well-served by the frappe pattern — a small `path → screen` resolver backed by Moqui screen records, so storefront pages are ordinary screens with a publish flag rather than a parallel page tree. Dolibarr's split of "marketing pages (CMS)" vs "account surface (portal)" maps directly onto the BOSS split between Commerce (marketing/merchandising) and Commons (portal/login). Odoo's per-website QWeb overrides are too coupled to replicate; BOSS should instead resolve content per-channel through the channel record (§2).

## 5. Carts & checkout

| Reference app | What it is | Key files |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Odoo (cart) | Cart is the sale order itself (`sale_order` with `website_sale` fields: cart-quantity, pricelist, metadata); the cart controller is a 591-line module handling add/update/coupon/optional-product flows and re-rendering fragments; wishlist records `product.wishlist` merge into the cart at checkout | `odoo/addons/website_sale/controllers/cart.py`, `odoo/addons/website_sale/models/sale_order.py`, `odoo/addons/website_sale/models/product_wishlist.py` |
| Odoo (checkout flow) | Checkout is a **data-driven sequence**: `website.checkout.step` records (name, `step_href`, button labels, `show_in_breadcrumb`, sequence) resolved per website — the flow can be re-ordered or extended by configuration rather than code; each step has its own template file (address/cart/checkout/extra-info/payment/confirmation) | `odoo/addons/website_sale/models/website_checkout_step.py`, `odoo/addons/website_sale/templates/checkout/{address,cart,checkout,confirmation,extra_info,payment}_templates.xml` |
| Odoo (checkout extras) | `website_checkout_alert_mixin` injects warnings mid-flow (out-of-stock, tax changes); a separate `product.configurator` and `combo_configurator` controller replace the plain "add to cart" when a product needs configuration before it can be added | `odoo/addons/website_sale/models/website_checkout_alert_mixin.py`, `odoo/addons/website_sale/controllers/{product_configurator,combo_configurator}.py` |
| Odoo (payment bridge) | Checkout hands off to the `payment` addon's portal transaction flow (`payment.py` controller, `payment_transaction` model in website_sale); abandoned carts get recovery emails driven from `sale_order` | `odoo/addons/website_sale/controllers/payment.py`, `odoo/addons/website_sale/models/payment_transaction.py`, `odoo/addons/website_sale/models/res_config_settings.py` (abandoned-cart block) |
| gauzy | A dedicated `cart` NestJS plugin: `commerce-cart` + `commerce-cart-line` entities, a `commerce-checkout-session` entity with its own repository/service/controller (session-ized checkout), cart promotions (`commerce-cart-promotion`), shipping-method links, a `totals-calculator` service, and a `cart-checkout.registry.ts` pluggable checkout-provider registry | `packages/plugins/cart/src/lib/commerce-cart/`, `packages/plugins/cart/src/lib/commerce-checkout-session/`, `packages/plugins/cart/src/lib/totals/totals-calculator.ts`, `packages/plugins/cart/src/lib/checkout/cart-checkout.registry.ts` |
| ERPNext | **Absent** — cart doctypes deleted in v15 (`patches/v15_0/delete_ecommerce_doctypes.py`); `shopping_cart/` is an empty shell; nothing in frappe core implements a cart | `frappe/erpnext/erpnext/patches/v15_0/delete_ecommerce_doctypes.py` |
| Dolibarr / Carbon / metasfresh / AureusERP | **Absent** — no cart or checkout surface (AureusERP's website plugin has no cart/checkout controllers at all; Dolibarr's `public/ecommerce` dir does not exist) | — |

**BOSS implications.** Two architectures worth copying: Odoo's data-driven checkout steps (a BOSS `commerce.checkout.step` record per channel would let implementers add a gift-wrap or approval step without code) and gauzy's session-ized checkout (a `commerce-checkout-session` entity that survives cart edits across devices, with a pluggable provider registry). The cart-as-actual-order pattern (Odoo) is the right default for BOSS — a quote that becomes an order — but a separate session entity (gauzy) is needed for anonymous/pre-auth checkout so the quote isn't spammed with junk carts. Both point to a BOSS design where cart = Sales quote + a channel-scoped session wrapper, and checkout steps are data, not code.

**BOSS implications.** Three portal architectures appear: config-declared surfaces (frappe: portal_settings maps doctypes into a sidebar — the cheapest to reproduce in Moqui, where the portal screen can render any published screen), template-extended record pages (Odoo: each doctype's portal page inherits commerce buttons), and a fully separate customer app panel (AureusERP's Filament Customer panel). BOSS already has the right chassis: the portal is the "My Account" area of Commons, and Commerce contributes its faces (order history with reorder, wishlist, withdrawal requests) as screens into it — no second app needed. Access-token document sharing (Odoo `_document_check_access`) is worth keeping for guest order lookup, which pairs with the withdrawal form's email+ref validation (§6).

**BOSS implications.** The consistent model across Odoo and gauzy: shipping methods are data records scoped by geography (Odoo's zip-prefix/price-rule tables are the most granular) and quoted at checkout before becoming order content. Payment provider setup must stay centralized (as in Odoo: commerce inherits provider settings rather than duplicating them), with the Commerce app contributing only per-channel availability toggles — matching BOSS's Accounting-owned payment config (research-accounting.md §7). Tax display (inclusive/exclusive, reference price) is a channel-level presentation setting distinct from the rate tables Accounting owns.

## 10. Gaps & notables in the checkout

- **The storefront layer is thin in every reference.** ERPNext extracted its whole webshop to the separate `frappe/webshop` app and Shopify integration to `frappe/ecommerce_integrations` — neither is in this checkout, leaving `shopping_cart/` an empty shell and the storefront at static pages + portal. Odoo is the only checkout with a complete cart/checkout/storefront stack; gauzy's `cart` plugin is the only other real cart implementation.
- **Dolibarr ships no online store** (`htdocs/public/ecommerce` absent) — its `website/` module is a CMS site builder and `webportal/` is account self-service. But `htdocs/public/` is full of self-service intake pages (donations, eventorganization, fichinter, invoice, members, partnership, project, recruitment, ticket, onlinesign, payment): a pattern of public forms that create ERP records without a cart.
- **No marketplace connectors in community anywhere** — Odoo's Amazon/eBay connectors are Enterprise; ERPNext pushed Shopify out; Dolibarr's community modules are payment/compliance (stancer, helloasso, ksef, einvoicing). Channel integrations are an extension-point concern, not core.
- **AureusERP's `website` plugin is a portal, not a store**: page/partner resources, portal-access grant/revoke actions, a customer Filament panel (Login/Register/PasswordReset/Account) and a dashboard — but no cart, checkout, or product merchandising (that plugs into the `products` plugin via schema slots). The blogs plugin covers marketing content.
- **metasfresh and Carbon have no commerce at all** (no webshop modules, no storefront); Carbon is B2B-only by design; metasfresh's storefront would live in its (absent) webshop backend modules.
- **Cross-cutting patterns worth copying**: channel as a first-class record that all storefront config binds to (Odoo `website`); public taxonomy separate from internal (Odoo `product.public.category`); publish/visibility mixin on every merchandisable record; channel filters added to the shared order list instead of a separate order desk (Odoo `sale_order_views.xml`); checkout as data (`website.checkout.step` records per website); feeds as cacheable records with manual invalidation (`product.feed`); portal surfaces config-declared (frappe `portal_settings`/`portal_menu_item`) vs a full second app panel (AureusERP customer panel); public intake forms that create records directly (Dolibarr `htdocs/public/`).
- **Checkout path quirks**: frappe `web_template`/`web_page_block` builder doctypes and `path_resolver.py` URL dispatch; Odoo QWeb template inheritance for every storefront page (`website_sale/templates/`); Dolibarr webportal's abstract card/list controllers as a reusable portal surface pattern; gauzy checkout sessions with pluggable provider registry (`cart-checkout.registry.ts`).
